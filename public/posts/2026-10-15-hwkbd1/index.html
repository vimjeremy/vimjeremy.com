Introduction.

So during the past year I’ve been heading more and more into the split keyboard space, having built a choc Ferris Sweep and experimented with different keyboard layouts, colemak and dvorak respectively (i stuck with qwerty, though). Recently though, I stumbled upon this guy Joe Scotto and I got intrigued by the whole handwired keyboard thing. What’s crazy to me is that you can literally build a (100% usable) keyboard for usually under 30 bucks. I decided to look into it, and next thing I know, I’m making my own.

What I want from my keyboard.

Before you start buying anything, you need to have a plan. The first step usually is to see what you want from your keyboard. Firstly, wireless is a must. I need it to work seamlessly with both my PC and Mac mini. Second, I want it to not require home row mods. The sweep was a foolish decision because it has only 4 thumb keys. Finally, I want it to be split for ergonomics (and to place my tea somewhere 😄).

Planning it out

Deciding on a layout.

Taking inspiration from some of Scotto’s keyboards (especially the Scotto34 and Scotto44) and considering my requirements, I arrived on something like this (sorry for the light theme):

This fulfills all my layout requirements and seems to be easy to build. Finding a 3U keycap and stabilizer might be hard, though.

Choosing the switches and stabilizers.

I really like choc switches, but i’m just so used to MX, so MX it is. For the specific switch, I want something that sounds good and is linear. I went with the Gateron Milky Yellow Pros 🤤. They’re pre-lubed and have a deep sound profile. For the stabilizer, I had a singular Durock stab lying around (yes, I know I’m lucky), so I just bought a 3U wire.

The microcontroller.

My first thought was the Nice! Nano V2. Then, I realized I dont have the money for two and I dont want to join the keyboard into two like in some of Scotto’s keyboards, since I want to be able to adjust the distance freely. Then, I found that there were NiceNano compatible microcontrollers on Ali for dirt cheap with the same chipset (nRF52840) that are Pro-Micro compatible. Here’s a link to it. We need 2 and I heard they could come DOA so I took 5 both just in case and for future projects.

Miscellaneous parts.

For the miscellaneous parts we need: Diodes (1N4148), 16 gauge bare copper wire, heatshrink tubing, wires (24-28awg), rechargeable pouch batteries (i went with 2x120mAh), filament with a semi-high melting point (PETG is fine), and of course keycaps. With this the keyboard should have 2-3 weeks of battery life as shown here:

Closing thoughts.

Part 2 is going to be about making the case, plate and schematic. The products are on the way and the final cost for all materials was around 100 bucks but there’s a lot of excess including a bunch of wire, heatshrink, 3 microcontrollers, keycaps, switches, and diodes. The raw material cost is probably much less but I dont have the patience to calculate it. Thanks for stopping by!
